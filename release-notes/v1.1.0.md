# v1.1.0

Adds Theorem 8.5 of the paper *Lehmer's totient problem with fewer than sixteen prime factors*: pseudo-solutions
prime to 3 exist for every k ≥ 25 with the sign +1 and for every k ≥ 26 with the sign −1.

- `pseudo/companion_k25.txt`: odd integers x₁ < ⋯ < x₂₅, none divisible by 3, with gcd(xᵢ, xⱼ − 1) = 1 for all i, j and
  x₁⋯x₂₅ + 1 = 2∏(xᵢ − 1). The first nine entries are 5, 7, 13, 17, 19, 23, 25, 37, 119; the product has 262,353 digits.
- `pseudo/descent.py`, `pseudo/make_seeds.py`: the search that found it, a descent through the defects 2B − A that
  keeps the 10⁷ admissible children with the smallest defect at each step (2.2 h on 4 cores, `pseudo/logs/descent.log`).
- `pseudo/check_tuple.py`, `pseudo/check_gp.sh`: independent checks in Python and in PARI/GP.
- `lean/LehmerTotient/PseudoExtend.lean`: appending 2∏(xᵢ − 1) + 1 keeps the sign +1, and appending ∏xᵢ gives the sign −1.
- `lean/LehmerTotient/PseudoData.lean` (generated by `lean/scripts/gen_pseudo.py`): the kernel checks the tuple, and
  `companion_pseudo_prime_to_three` and `lehmer_pseudo_prime_to_three` give every k ≥ 25 and every k ≥ 26.
  `lean/Check.lean` now lists 52 theorems, all depending only on Lean's standard axioms.

Some entries are composite (25, 119 = 7·17), so none of these tuples is a solution of φ(n) | n ∓ 1. Neither Lehmer's
totient conjecture nor the question whether φ(n) | n+1 has further solutions is settled.
